How to choose a pre-roll tube size.

Tube size is a smaller decision than it looks — but get it wrong and you're either jamming a cone that doesn't fit or paying to ship air. The size is driven by one thing: the cone you're filling. Everything else (the CR closure, the cert, the colors) stays the same across sizes. So pick the cone, and the tube follows.

The four sizes that matter

Why 116mm dominates

King-size became the default consumer format in most adult-use markets, and the 116mm tube is built for exactly that cone — 116mm (4.57") tall, 21mm OD, one cone up to 1.5g with label room. Because it's the standard, it has the deepest, most reliable supply and the best per-unit pricing. If you don't have a specific reason to go smaller or larger, 116mm is the safe, liquid default. It's the only size Pack Guys stocks — one spec, done deeply, in five colors.

How size moves your cost

Two cost levers ride on size:

  • Per-unit price. 116mm is the highest-volume size in the category, so it carries the tightest pricing. Off-standard sizes (70mm, 120mm) are lower-volume and usually cost more per unit and take longer to source.
  • MOQ math. Every size is a separate SKU with its own minimum. Run two sizes and you split your volume across two minimums — which can knock both onto a worse price tier. Posted 116mm tiers run $0.085/unit at 1K cases down to $0.060 at 50K; see the live catalog for every tier.

Want the landed math for your case size and ship-to ZIP? Run it on the true-cost calculator — it shows per-unit and lead time across sourcing paths in about 30 seconds.

Picking, in three questions

  1. What cone are you filling? King-size → 116mm. Half-gram → 90mm. Mini → 70mm. Fat/long → 120mm.
  2. Do you actually need a second size? If one format covers most of your line, standardize on it and keep your volume on one tier.
  3. Does the size carry deep supply? A size you can reorder same-week beats a size you have to wait 8–12 weeks for. 116mm wins this on availability.
