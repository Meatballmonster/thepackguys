CR packaging requirements by state.

Here's the thing most operators get backwards: the pre-roll child-resistant tube barely changes state to state. The label does. The closure is governed by one federal test (16 CFR § 1700.20, classified under ASTM D3475) that every state below incorporates by reference. So you buy one spec — a 116mm CR tube — and swap the state warning label on top. That's the whole game.

This page is the index: 20 markets, the regulator, the statute, and the one or two finish rules that actually differ. Each state links to a full compliance summary with the warning-copy specifics. Informational, not legal advice — confirm current rules with the state regulator before you print.

The baseline every state shares

  • CR closure tested per 16 CFR § 1700.20 (≤20% of children 42–51 months can open; ≥90% of adults can). Same federal bar everywhere.
  • A lab CR cert PDF on file with the licensee, naming the closure design, protocol, lab, date, and batch.
  • A tamper-evident seal at the point of sale.
  • A state warning + cannabis symbol + THC content on the retail label — this is the part that varies.

The differences that actually trip people up

  • Resealable / lifetime-CR. California, Colorado, and Massachusetts spell out that the tube must stay child-resistant for every open-and-close cycle, not just first opening. A squeeze pop-top passes this because there's no hinge to wear out.
  • Conditional opacity (B2B-friendly). Oklahoma requires opacity only after sale — clear tubes are fine for B2B shipment, and the retailer applies the opaque overlay. New Jersey and Florida allow the same B2B / co-pack pattern. This is why clear tubes still sell into those markets.
  • Child-appeal restrictions. Massachusetts and New York explicitly bar packaging that appeals to children — no cartoons, no fruit imagery. Keep brand art adult-coded.
  • Tracking tags. Oklahoma calls out Metrc RFID at the case level plus a batch number on every retail package.
  • Single-portion exit packaging. New York requires individual pre-rolls to leave the store in single-portion exit packaging.
  • Medical-only markets. Florida and Pennsylvania run medical-only programs at the time of writing (confirm current program status) — same CR rules, dispensed under the medical program.

What this means for sourcing

One spec covers all 20. A 116mm CR squeeze pop-top with a lab cert PDF and an opaque option clears the packaging bar in every market on this list — you handle the rest at the label layer. For multi-state brands, that's the cheapest path: pick one tube, stock it deep, swap the warning per state. Full procurement detail is in the Operator Guide; the CR test itself is broken down in the CR cert deep-dive.
