White-label & custom-printed tubes.

You started on stock colors because they ship same-week and carry the deepest supply. At some point the brand outgrows a bare tube — you want your logo on the body, a color that isn't one of the four, a tube that looks like yours on the shelf. That's white-label. Same 116mm CR spec, your branding on it. Here's exactly how it works, what it costs, and the one compliance question to settle before you sign a PO.

Stock vs. custom — when to make the jump

Custom is a real step up in cost and calendar, so it's worth doing for the right reason, not just because it's available. The honest split:

You should stay on stock if…Custom is worth it when…
You're testing a SKU or a market and volume is still unprovenThe brand is established and a reorder cadence is predictable
You need packaging this week to keep flower movingYou can plan a few weeks ahead of your fill date
One of the four stock colors already fits the lookShelf differentiation or brand recognition is driving real sell-through
Per-unit budget is tight on this single runThe shelf lift is worth a custom run's premium over stock

What you can customize

Two levers, and they're priced differently:

  • Custom color. A tube body color beyond the stock Clear / Black / White / Smoke, matched to your brand spec. Send your target color — a Pantone reference is ideal — and we confirm the match and any finish limits on your quote.
  • Custom print / branding. Your logo and artwork on the tube body. Send your art and we confirm the decoration method, print colors, and usable print area for the 116mm body on your quote.

The closure stays the same molded squeeze pop-top across every option — custom changes the look of the body, not the child-resistant mechanism that carries the cert.

The terms — what a custom run costs and takes

Custom tubes are made to order, not pulled from the LA shelf, so the numbers differ from posted stock tiers — but the minimum doesn't. Custom runs start at the same 1,000-unit case as stock, where most suppliers gate custom at 25K+. Current custom terms:

Does printing keep the CR cert? — settle this first

This is the question that actually matters, and the one a compliance buyer asks before anything else. The child-resistant certification is carried by the closure design — the squeeze pop-top tested under ASTM D3475 + 16 CFR § 1700.20. Decorating the tube body does not touch that closure mechanism, so a printed tube generally ships against the same cert as the stock tube.

"Generally" is not "always," so get it in writing for your run — ask for the CR cert PDF and confirm it covers your decorated SKU, not just the base closure. A real supplier states this plainly and hands the cert PDF over; if a custom quote goes quiet on the cert, that's the same red flag as any other tube supplier.

How to start — sample first, then custom quote

  1. Order a stock sample. Confirm the 116mm body, closure, and color feel before you commit to a branded run. A $14.99 sample case credits to your first wholesale order.
  2. Send your art + target spec. Logo, color target, and the rough volume you'd run. The faster you nail the spec, the faster the few-week clock starts.
  3. Get the custom quote + cert confirmation. Posted-style numbers for your MOQ, plus the CR-cert answer above in writing.
  4. Run it. Approve a proof, then the made-to-order run ships against your terms — Net-30 on the same schedule as stock accounts.
